**Forecast-driven arbitrage in the Spanish day-ahead market, a reinforcement-learning comparison and an investment valuation**

## The question

How much does a 1 MW / 2 MWh battery earn by buying cheap midday solar power and selling it in the evening peak? How much of that depends on machine-learning price forecasts? And what installed cost does that justify for an investor?

## Headline results (test period 1 Jan 2019 – 25 Nov 2026)

| | Net margin (€/MW) | Capture of perfect foresight |
|---|---|---|
| Perfect foresight (upper bound) | 251.595 | 100,0% |
| **Ensemble forecast (LEAR + LightGBM) + optimiser** | **209.204** | **83,2%** |
| Yesterday's prices + optimiser | 187.492 | 74,5% |
| Clock schedule | 164.119 | 65,2% |

- **Forecasts.** The ensemble cuts the forecast error by 18,5% against yesterday's prices (MAE 13,80 vs 16,93 €/MWh). Its daily-profit gain is significant against every alternative (Diebold–Mariano t from 3,8 to 13,6).
- **Regimes.** Capture rises from 47% (2019–2020) to 89% (2023–2026) as solar deepens the daily price curve. In 2025 the battery earned €49.149 per MW net of wear.
- **Reinforcement learning.** A tabular Q-learning agent given the same forecast captures 77%, no better than the clock schedule. With known physics, optimisation wins.
- **Valuation.** Day-ahead arbitrage alone justifies an installed cost of about **€143–183 per kWh** at a 10% cost of capital (15 years, 3% annual spread compression).

## Reproduce

**Colab (no setup):** open `notebooks/capstone_bess_spain.ipynb` and run all (about 6 minutes). The price data are stored in the notebook.

**Locally:**
```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python run.py --fresh      # recomputes the walk-forward forecasts (~4 min), then every table and figure
python run.py              # reuses results/forecasts.pkl if present
```
Every table goes to `results/t*.csv`, every figure to `results/figures/f*.png`, and every number quoted in the manuscript to `results/key_numbers.json`.

## Design choices that prevent look-ahead

- The schedule for day D uses only information available at noon of D−1, the gate closure of the day-ahead market.
- Every model is refitted monthly on data strictly before the month it forecasts.
- Hyperparameters were chosen on 2017–2018. The 2019–2026 test period was never used for any choice.
- Scaling and median/MAD normalisation use the calibration window only.
